Ecommerce Analytics: A Practical Guide to Connecting Store, Ad, and Finance Data

HologrowUpdated October 12, 202614 min read

Start ecommerce analytics with five decisions: whether sales are growing, orders are profitable, acquisition is affordable, conversion is healthy, and customers return. Combine order, advertising, and cost records using consistent definitions. For recurring cross-source questions, evaluate a preparation layer such as Hologrow after verifying the exact evidence your decision requires.

TL;DR

  • Track net sales, contribution dollars, acquisition spend, conversion, and repeat purchasing before adding secondary metrics.
  • Use orders for sales, advertising reports for spend, and effective-dated costs for contribution. Never add attributed sales to order revenue.
  • For questions spanning supported commerce, advertising, ERP, and analytics sources, Hologrow is a data-preparation option to evaluate against the exact accounts, tables, dates, and fields you need.
  • Start with one weekly decision and a reconciled worksheet. Automate only after the totals and definitions agree.

Which ecommerce numbers should I review every week?

Start with net merchandise sales, contribution dollars, advertising spend, conversion, and repeat purchasing. Use these to decide whether growth is valuable; add operational metrics only when they explain a material change.

The useful starting point is not “How many metrics can we track?” It is “What would we do differently if we knew the answer?” A US merchant selling on Amazon and a direct-to-consumer store might need to fund a promotion, replenish a product, or investigate a deteriorating margin. Those decisions need different inputs.

For a promotion review, begin with fulfilled demand, discounts, returns, product costs, and incremental marketing expense. For a checkout investigation, start with sessions and funnel events. For a cash question, examine payment and settlement timing. Putting all three on one chart does not make them interchangeable.

Write a decision contract: “On Monday, the growth lead will decide whether to maintain the current promotion using two complete weeks of net sales and variable contribution.” Name an owner, a cutoff, and the evidence that would change the decision. This is the first deliverable; the dashboard comes later.

Which data do you need from stores, advertising, and finance?

For a basic cross-channel review, you need order lines, daily advertising expense, product costs, and refunds or financial adjustments. Preserve account, date, currency, and product identifiers so those inputs can be reconciled.

Use a small data contract before connecting more systems. The fields below describe a recommended analytical model, not a promise that every connector exposes these exact column names. Map them to the actual schema and preserve source identifiers.

An order line and a campaign-day are different units of observation, often called their grain. Never join every order directly to every campaign on date alone. Ten order lines and three campaigns can produce thirty rows. Aggregate first to a justified shared level, such as channel and business day, or use an explicitly documented allocation method.

How do you reconcile revenue without counting it twice?

Choose the order-based revenue measure for commercial reporting, then explain how ad-attributed sales and payouts differ. Do not average the three or force them to match; each answers a different question.

Give each revenue measure a separate name. Store net merchandise sales answer an order-economics question. An advertising platform’s attributed sales answer a credit-allocation question. Payouts answer a cash-timing question. Add neither ad-attributed sales to store sales nor payouts to revenue.

For this management view, define net merchandise sales as gross merchandise sales less discounts and returns, excluding tax and shipping revenue. This is a chosen convention, not a universal platform or accounting definition. Keep shipping revenue and related costs as explicit lines when material. Finance should approve the convention before it becomes recurring.

Use three reconciliation tests: totals before versus after joins; missing or duplicated business keys; and date boundaries around refunds and cancellations. A reconciliation bridge can contain an unexplained residual. Showing that residual is better than forcing agreement through an undocumented adjustment.

How do I reconcile a sales report in a spreadsheet before connecting more tools?

Build a four-column worksheet: source amount, included adjustments, excluded items, and explained difference. Start with a closed period from one store. This is the fastest way to discover whether you have a business discrepancy or only two differently named metrics.

For a synthetic example, suppose gross merchandise sales are $130,000, discounts are $8,000, and merchandise sales reversals are $5,000. Net merchandise sales are $117,000. If your comparison report also includes $6,000 of tax and $4,000 of net shipping charges, its total becomes $127,000 under these simplified assumptions. The $10,000 difference does not mean ten thousand dollars of orders are missing.

The terminology matters. Shopify’s sales report definitions distinguish net sales from total sales and now use sales reversals for adjustments broader than physical returns. Match the report’s actual fields; do not mechanically subtract both an old returns column and its replacement.

Next, test the scope. Are both reports limited to the same store and sales channels? Are canceled or unpaid orders treated the same way? Did either export include an incomplete day? Write each adjustment on its own line so the reconciliation remains explainable next month.

For website revenue, make a separate comparison rather than changing the order ledger. Google documents purchase deduplication through transaction IDs for web streams. Missing, repeated, or empty IDs warrant a tracking investigation. A tag problem should be repaired at collection, not hidden by multiplying analytics revenue until it matches sales.

Finish by selecting five ordinary orders and a few exceptions—such as a refund, a discount, and an edited order—and tracing them through the worksheet. If the sample reconciles but the aggregate does not, inspect excluded records and grouping rules. If neither reconciles, repair the definition before building additional dashboards. This small exercise establishes a reusable acceptance test for any later connector or AI workflow.

What does a connected profit analysis look like?

Calculate contribution from an explicit sales definition and non-overlapping cost lines, then show both dollars and margin. A positive revenue trend is not enough if the added orders contribute less after variable costs.

Consider this synthetic monthly example in USD. Costs follow the same management reporting convention; these are not customer results or an audited income statement.

Illustrative contribution bridge—not customer data

Net merchandise sales$120,000
Product costs−$42,000
Variable fulfillment and transaction costs−$18,000
Advertising−$30,000
Contribution before fixed overhead$30,000

Contribution is $120,000 − $42,000 − $18,000 − $30,000 = $30,000, or 25% of net merchandise sales. If the cost file covers only 80% of units, do not publish that margin as complete. Show the covered share, uncosted units, and a clearly labeled estimate or range.

Next investigate volume, net price, product mix, unit cost, fulfillment, and advertising. Aggregate totals point to an investigation; they do not establish causation. A cheaper product mix can lower average order value even when no individual product’s price changes.

How do I connect Amazon, store, and ad data to Hologrow for AI analysis?

Connect each supported source in Hologrow Integrations, wait for the required tables to finish syncing, verify account coverage and dates in Data Explorer, and only then authorize an AI client. Amazon Seller and Amazon Ads require separate connections; a successful login alone does not prove that the data needed for your question is ready.

Start with a source plan, not the Connect button. Write down the business question, the accounts it covers, the reporting period, and the evidence required. A contribution question may need Amazon Seller orders, Amazon Ads performance, fees, returns, product costs, and perhaps Lingxing ERP. A website-acquisition question may need a supported store plus Google Ads, Meta Ads, or GA4. Check the current Hologrow Integrations directory before promising a workflow: connection availability and table coverage depend on the platform, permissions, and workspace. If a required platform is marked Coming soon, do not describe it as connected.

Next, sign in at Hologrow and open Integrations. Find the source, click Connect, or use Manage when adding another account. For Amazon Seller (3P), choose North America, Europe, or Far East, optionally add a note that identifies the business or marketplace group, and authorize the intended Seller Central account. Amazon Vendor (1P) uses its own integration. For Amazon Ads, repeat the process from the Amazon Ads integration, choose the correct region, sign in through Login with Amazon, and approve access to the required advertising profiles. The Amazon Ads setup guide states that Seller Central authorization does not replace advertising authorization. Connect additional regions separately.

After authorization, return to Manage and confirm the intended account is listed. If Hologrow asks you to start syncing, review the data settings and requested date range before starting. “Connected” means the authorization is valid; it does not mean the historical backfill has finished. This distinction matters when an AI answer appears to omit an older marketplace, campaign, or order period.

Open Data Hub → Data Explorer and use it as the acceptance test. The Data Explorer guide says each store or ad account shows its platform, status, last-synced time, and account details. Wait for the relevant account to reach Synced. Expand its history to check the latest successful run, duration, progress, and rows synced. Under Edit, inspect detailed table usage: table name, row count, and the earliest and latest available dates. Do not start a 12-month comparison when the required table contains only 30 days. Large initial backfills can take time, while scheduled incremental syncs continue in the background.

Then inspect the Hologrow data schema. Confirm that the required datasets exist at the right grain and can be related through maintained business keys. Orders, daily campaign performance, SKU costs, settlements, and inventory are different datasets; connecting accounts does not automatically create a valid profit definition or supply missing landed-cost history. Record which source owns revenue, spend, refunds, fees, and product cost before asking AI to join or compare them.

Only after those checks should you connect an AI client. In Hologrow, open AI Hub → AI Platforms. The AI Platforms guide recommends OAuth for supported clients such as Codex or Claude; click the platform, choose Connect, and complete authorization. For a standard MCP client, copy Hologrow's hosted MCP server URL and create a revocable MCP key only when the client requires one. Treat the key like a password, never paste it into a chat, and rotate or revoke it when a device or client is retired. Hologrow's access is read-only: it does not change campaigns, bids, budgets, listings, prices, inventory, or orders.

For ChatGPT, follow the official ChatGPT setup guide: use ChatGPT in a web browser, enable Developer mode if your plan and workspace permit it, create a custom MCP app named Hologrow, enter https://mcp.hologrow.ai/mcp as the server URL, choose OAuth, and sign in with Hologrow. For Claude web or Desktop, the Claude setup guide directs you to Settings → Customize → Connectors, where you can find Hologrow, add it, and approve access. Workspace administrators may need to allow custom connectors.

Finish with a two-stage verification. First ask the AI to list the connected platforms or available accounts without performing analysis. Confirm that the names match Data Explorer. Then run one closed-period reconciliation: request source totals, table names, date coverage, freshness, currency, and any missing cost fields before requesting explanations. Compare the result with an exported source report. If totals disagree, fix scope, time zone, attribution, or join logic before building a WBR, profitability model, or automated report.

Troubleshoot from the source outward. If Data Explorer shows Error or Needs reauth, repair authorization in Integrations and confirm the next sync succeeds. If Amazon data is missing, check the seller or advertising account, marketplace, selected region, profile access, sync start, and requested date range. Disconnecting a source stops future syncs and deletes that connection's synchronized data, so export or confirm retention needs before removing it.

How should you ask AI for a source-backed answer?

Ask for an evidence table and calculation before an explanation. Specify accounts, complete dates, currency, metric definitions, and missing-data handling so the answer can be reproduced by someone outside the chat.

Run two passes. First validate scope and arithmetic. Then request explanations the evidence supports. Reject an answer that turns correlation into causation or substitutes attributed sales for actual orders.

A weekly review needs four outputs: a decision, its evidence, an unresolved question, and a named next action. Preserve that packet so the following week starts from consistent definitions rather than another debate about what “revenue” means.

A question to ask your AI

For our US business, compare the last two complete reporting weeks. First list accounts, table coverage, time zones, and freshness. Reconcile net merchandise sales before analyzing contribution. State the cost definition and uncosted share. Return the three largest changes with sources, calculations, uncertainty, and the next evidence needed. Do not modify source accounts.

Customer questions, answered

Do I need a data warehouse before using ecommerce analytics?

No. A small operation can start with controlled exports and a documented spreadsheet. Consider a warehouse or managed preparation layer when repeated joins, account volume, history, or access control make that process unreliable. The trigger is the work you need to maintain, not a universal revenue threshold or an obligation to adopt a particular technology.

How often should I refresh a weekly business report?

Refresh often enough to support the decision, but review complete periods and record when the source last changed. Daily preparation can support a weekly meeting without making every metric final immediately. Freeze a reviewed snapshot for the meeting and note later revisions. A daily dashboard is not evidence that refunds, attribution, and source reports have all settled.

What should I do when my product costs are incomplete?

Report sales and known expenses, then show the uncosted products and their share of units or revenue. You can publish a clearly labeled scenario using an explicit cost assumption, but do not label it realized margin. Prioritize missing costs on the products that could change the decision; filling every minor record first may delay the useful answer.
